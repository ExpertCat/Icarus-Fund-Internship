# Icarus-Fund-Internship
Researched cross-sectional momentum in equity-options markets by building a robust no-lookahead Python backtesting pipeline for monthly delta-neutral straddle strategies using CRSP-style market data, with a historical annual Sharpe of 1.22

I did an options research internship at Icarus Fund, focusing on the delta-neutral straddle strategy. We mainly examine whether stocks with quite strong recent straddle returns continue to outperform those with weak ones, based on the most recent year’s performance. We used OptionMetrics daily option quotes from Jan 2018 to Dec 2023. Each monthly portfolio is formed on the third Friday of the month and expires on the third Friday of the next month, creating contracts with roughly one month to expiration.

First, we use a formula (liquidity score = total open interest / (1 + med_spread)) to sort secid so that we can select the top 20 secid by liquidity. This automatically favors securities with a narrow spread and a higher open interest. Before constructing each option straddle, we first filter the option contracts: we require both calls and puts to have positive open interest, and the calls must have a delta between 0.25 and 0.75. When pairing the straddle, the puts must have the same security identifier, expiration date, and strike price as the calls. The reason for restricting the delta range is to avoid extremely deep ITM and OTM contracts, as they could be very illiquid or unrepresentative (some option contracts could be very cheap because they are very OTM, but if they turn out to be ITM, the monthly return percentage profit would be huge). To keep the straddle delta neutral, we can use calculations to give weights to the call and the put. Weight of Call = -Delta Put / (Delta Call - Delta Put), Weight of Put = 1 - Weight of Call, because we assume the sum of the weights adds up to 1.

Then, within the 20 security identifiers we previously selected, we select the most liquid matched straddle per secid by first minimizing the combined spread and using entry_mid to tie break. Combined spread = call spread + put spread. Entry mid = Call’s weight * call_mid + Put’s weight * put_mid. Up to this point, we have 20 straddles per month, with one straddle per security identifier. Then, we calculate the final payoff for each straddle, the exit value of delta-neutral straddles equals Weight of Call * Payoff of Call + Weight of Put * Payoff of Put. This then can be easily converted to a monthly realized return by (final payoff of the straddle - the entry mid) / entry mid.

After computing the monthly payoff percentage, we start constructing the signals. The past 12 months’ return of each permno (stock) is extracted, and the most recent month’s return percentage is excluded to avoid unstable microstructures. Hence, for the remaining 11 months, we average the monthly percentage return and rank them to form a cross-sectional ranking (comparing different stocks within the same month). We use MOM_rt variable to denote momentum return, if the stocks with fewer than 8 inside the 11 months window, MOM_rt is set to NaN so that we could avoid signals driven by sparse histories. Since we average the past 11 month’s return percentage to treat it as the momentum signal, it could be hugely dominated by a single extreme option. Hence, we provide a winsorization control by setting a lower capping bound at -0.95 and a higher capping bound at 5.0 to lower the impact of those extreme outliers. Then the stocks are separated into quintiles by their momentum. The quintile with the highest momentum will be used for long positions, and the bottom quintile will be used for shorting. 
 
Overall, we have three strategies, the first is the one we just mentioned, go long for the top quintile and go short for the stocks in the bottom quintile. The second strategy is similar, but it takes only the top 3 stocks in momentum ranking to go long and the bottom 3 to go short. The third is an early-exit strategy based on the top 3 and bottom 3; it will exit the straddle if the position lost 75% of its entry value or gained 80% of its entry value.

To evaluate each strategy, we calculated monthly returns for the long winner portfolio, the short loser portfolio, and a combined portfolio with 50% weighting on the long side and the short side. We compared the strategies using average monthly return, annualized return, annualized volatility, Sharpe ratio, and the t-statistic of the mean return. Annualized return was estimated by scaling the average monthly return to 12 months. Annualized volatility was obtained by scaling monthly volatility by the square root of 12. The Sharpe ratio measured return relative to risk, and the t-statistic provided an indicator of whether the average return was meaningfully different from zero. 

Besides the normal weighting for the long bucket and the short bucket stocks, we also tried using inverse volatility weighting to assign the weights. Within each of the three strategies, we computed the volatility of the selected stocks and assigned less weight to the ones with higher volatility and more weight to the ones with lower volatility. These weights are then normalized to fit the strategy. The transaction costs are treated as we buy at a price a little higher than the quoted price and received a little less when selling. As one basis point is 0.01%, the transaction cost is straddle entry value multiplied by 2*x/10000 (fixed transaction cost rate), assuming x is the fixed basis points cost per leg.

In total, we have three strategies combined with two weighting methods (6 specifications): 
1) quintile bucket + even weighting within long/short
2) top 3/bottom 3 + even weighting within long/short
3) early exit + top 3/bottom 3 + even weighting withinlong/short
4) quintile bucket + inverse volatility weighting within long/short
5) top 3/bottom 3 + inverse volatility weighting within long/short
6) early exit + top 3/bottom 3 + inverse volatility weighting of long/short

All 6 produced Sharpe ratios greater than 1 (refer to the results pptx).

Across 54 months, the 50/50 long-short portfolio generated an average monthly return of 19.76%, monthly volatility of 56.26%, an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.22, and a t-statistic of 2.58 under the assumption that monthly returns are independent and similarly distributed, providing statistically significant evidence that the average return is positive. The arithmetic annualized return was 237.15%, which is calculated by multiplying the average monthly return by 12. Monthly returns ranged from −77.13% to 204.80%, indicating a strong performance but also the potential risk of fluctuation.
